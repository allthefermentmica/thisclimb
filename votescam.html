Births and abortions are reported counts; the remainder is calculated, assuming half of conceptions are lost without an abortion, a mid-range estimate.20 Where abortion is rarer, the spontaneous-to-abortion ratio is higher because the number of abortions is small, not because more conceptions are lost. Faith and the law

God gave the commandment “Do not kill” (Exodus 20:13).31 Jewish law, as recorded in the Talmud, does not treat a fetus as a person until birth. Yevamot 69b calls it “mere water” for the first forty days, and Rashi on Sanhedrin 72b says it is not a person until born.25,28 Throughout pregnancy, Jewish teaching treats the fetus as part of its mother’s body until birth.25,33 Some rabbis describe the moment of personhood as the first breath, pointing to Genesis 2:7, where God “breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.”26,27,32,31 The Hebrew text of Exodus 21:22–23 punishes causing a miscarriage with a fine; “life for life” applies only if further harm follows. The Greek Septuagint reads differently, applying “life for life” if the fetus is fully formed.29

The idea that life begins at conception arose after Jesus lived, so it cannot be shown to be something he believed, and the Gospels record no teaching from him on when life begins.31 Several Church Fathers instead held that the soul arrives later, at 30 to 90 days, a view that remained valid through the Middle Ages. Immediate ensoulment at fertilization originated in the 15th century and, in one history’s account, became Catholic dogma in 1869.24 Catholic sources add that the 1869 declaration left the timing of ensoulment aside.34

How abortion became a mobilizing issue

After the civil rights movement, the Republican Party needed a voter base that did not depend on open appeals to segregation.35,40 Historians describe how New Right strategists such as Paul Weyrich and Richard Viguerie built that base in the 1970s around social issues.37 Randall Balmer traces the Religious Right’s organizing to the defense of segregation academies and argues that abortion was adopted later because it could mobilize evangelical voters at scale.35,36 A 1982 analysis described the plan: make abortion and other social issues salient, enlist single-issue groups, and get voters to vote on the issue rather than on party.38 Kristin Kobes Du Mez dates abortion’s rise as a mobilizing force to 1979, when Weyrich identified it as a way to build the Moral Majority.39

Church-goers are consistent voters, and a voter who is told that one issue decides everything has no reason to look at anything else. You are supposed to be a one-issue voter so that you don’t look into the policies that eliminate services and start everlasting wars. People have been manipulated to vote against their own interests in the name of a problem that is not being addressed honestly.
